# Reading the findings

Start with a law in the collection. Its card gives the number of differences in meaning (a link to the reader), whether the review is complete and how many languages were reviewed, and an impact bar whose legend counts Major, Limited and No impact rating yet. The card also names the reviewed source version and, where a paid ledger covers the act, its metered AI processing cost; Review and sources explains what that amount includes. These are different measures. A review can be complete without finding a difference, and a published finding can still lack an impact rating.

The language count includes only non-English versions fully compared with English. Partly checked versions and failed source retrievals remain in the total but do not count as reviewed. Incomplete describes the saved coverage; it does not mean work is currently running. Review and sources gives the details.

Open the law to see its English text beside a list of findings. Select highlighted wording or an item in the list to compare the language versions. Each language starts with its name, any impact rating and a link to its official source. The official English quotation sits directly above the AI translation of that language, with differing words shaded in both; the official original follows, then the explanation of the possible difference in meaning. A potential impact section, where available, gives the effect if the reading prevails, the reasoning and its uncertainty; assessment details and the link to the whole issue are folded away. English provides a common reference; it has no higher authority than the other official versions.

## Narrow the comparison

Language and impact filters work together. The language menu lists versions with matching findings. Major and Limited select assigned binary assessments; No impact rating yet includes findings without an assigned level. This last category can include an unresolved assessment and does not imply a limited effect.

The list groups records that share an English passage and quotation. Its heading counts both, for example “24 differences in meaning at 13 places in the text”: language counts still count the original finding records, and each place is one shared English quotation. The arrows move between those places. Copy link preserves the selected comparison and filters.

Text order follows the source. Potential impact opens an issue-level view, where one issue can link several language-specific findings. A single note states that ratings are provisional AI assessments; an issue in any other state keeps its own label. A rating describes a possible change in legal outcome if a reading prevails, with its confidence and uncertainty shown separately. Earlier three-tier assessments retain their explanations as awaiting reassessment.

## Read the evidence in context

The review status sits beside the source version at the top of the reader; the version disclosure holds its scope notes. Review and sources provides the exact version, source links, archives and coverage notes. A completed label refers to the preserved source scope. Missing languages, mixed-language official files and unfinished comparisons remain explicit. A source import checks currency at that point in time; the project does not continuously monitor later legal changes.

Highlights reproduce exact substrings. When the same words occur several times, the reader can highlight several matches; this does not establish a separate discrepancy at every occurrence. The source itself is never rewritten to make a comparison clearer.

The collection's language summary compares only languages available in every fully reviewed act in its stated scope. It is a count within this collection, not a measure of overall translation quality. No finding should be read as an adjudicated legal error.

The website works while the research computer is offline. Anyone with the link can read and forward it. Search-engine exclusion is requested, but the link is not an access restriction.
