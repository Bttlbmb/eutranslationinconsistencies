Method

The English text is a reading reference. All official language versions have equal authority. Choose a focus language to see only its published differences and English-text highlights. Select highlighted text to see original wording, an English translation, and the potential difference.

Focus impact narrows the findings and highlights to an assigned potential-impact level. It can be combined with Focus language. No impact rating yet means no level has been assigned for that finding; an issue explanation may be under review. It does not mean Limited impact. The focus is kept in shared links.

Published findings are supported by independent model analyses and evidence review. They identify possible differences in meaning, not proof that a translation is legally wrong. An act with no findings is not necessarily equivalent across languages; check its review coverage and source version.

One comparison unit means one English passage checked against one other language.

Language findings are published finding records. Grouped comparisons combine records with the same English quotation in the same passage; a group may contain several distinct issues. Underlining in the comparison marks wording differences between the English reference and a translation into English. It is a reading aid, not an impact rating.

Potential impact proposals describe the possible practical effect if a reading prevails. MVP ratings are preliminary estimates, without a separate editorial approval. Issues needing more context have no rating. They are grouped by legal issue, which may span several highlighted passages. A shared English passage may contain several distinct issues. An act without impact proposals is not assessed as having no impact.

The potential impact scale applies to the possible legal outcome:

  • High potential impact: A substantial outcome for a broad class, several independent rules, or a serious consequence under one rule, supported by evidence of reach or consequence.
  • Moderate: A plausible changed outcome for an identifiable group under a specific rule.
  • Limited: A changed outcome confined to an ancillary detail with limited practical consequence.
